about this project

Written, coded, and illustrated by Christine Nguyen.

This project is an interactive, choice-based website that simulates how life experiences across four developmental stages—childhood (ages 0-10), early adolescence (ages 11-14), late adolescence (15-18), and early adulthood (19-25)—can influence criminal behavior.

(The reason the stages are laid out as such is because crime peaks in late adolescence to early adulthood, so mid- to later-adulthood is not explored.)

The simulation is grounded primarily in Hirschi’s Social Bond Theory (1969), with Agnew’s General Strain Theory (1992) as a complementary framework.

Thus, each choice strengthens or weakens key social bonds and alters levels of strain. The final outcome reflects how cumulative social bonds and strain shape conformity or deviance.

To make these outcomes deterministic for the purpose of this game, the player’s scores are measured in five different dimensions: attachment, commitment, involvement, belief, and strain. These scores have neutral default values at the beginning of playthrough, and by the end, each choice the player makes will have modified these scores. The resulting tally dictates what ending the player receives.
