Agnew's general strain theory

According to Agnew, strain arises from:

  • Failure to achieve positive goals.
  • The removal of positive stimuli (family, friends, a source of income, etc).
  • The presence of negative stimuli (abusive family members, authority figures, punishments, etc).

Strain generates negative emotions such as anger, frustration, and resentment. When positive coping resources are limited (when social bonds are weak) individuals may turn to crime instead as a coping mechanism.

In this project, strain accumulates through participation in antisocial behavior and rejection of social norms. (Because this game so heavily depends on the player's choices, we focus more on internal sources of strain and less on external sources of strain, outside of our control.) High strain amplifies the negative effects of weak social bonds.
