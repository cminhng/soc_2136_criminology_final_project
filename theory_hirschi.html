Hirschi's social bond theory

Hirschi argues that individuals refrain from crime not because of inherent morality, but because they are bonded to society. Therefore, crime becomes more likely when these bonds are weakened or broken.

Hirschi identifies four elements of the social bond:

  • Attachment
    • The emotional and psychological ties to others, such as parents, caregivers, teachers, and peers. Strong attachment is associated with a desire to live up to expectations and gain approval.
    • When attachment is weak, rule-breaking or deviant behavior is more likely due to lack of concern for others’ opinions.
    • In this project, attachment is shaped by early family relationships (outside of the player’s control), the presence or absence of mentors, and acceptance or rejection of support offered by others.
  • Commitment
    • An individual’s investment in conventional goals. Eg. education, reputation, and future success. People with strong commitment have more to lose if they engage in criminal behavior.
    • Low commitment reduces the perceived cost of deviance.
    • In this project, commitment is influenced by academic engagement, long-term goal formation, and early career stability.
  • Involvement
    • Participation in structured activities such as school, sports, work, or community organizations. Time spent in prosocial activities reduces opportunities for deviant behavior.
    • In this project, involvement is affected by participation or lack of participation in non-criminal structured or time-consuming activities.
  • Belief
    • Internalized moral values and acceptance of social norms and laws. Individuals who believe rules are legitimate are less likely to violate them.
    • In this project, belief is shaped by exposure to role models, goal-setting, and the normalization or rejection of antisocial behavior.
